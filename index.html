One theory, from before the Big Bang

Humans have always lived by models of reality: myths, religions, philosophies, sciences. Each explains part of the world well. None has explained all of it in one theory that holds together from start to finish. HMR is an attempt at that theory.

A partial model explains one part and leaves the rest as given. A holistic model explains everything it covers from the same starting point, so nothing is left as a loose end. A blueprint is a holistic model of a building. HMR tries the same idea with all of reality as the thing modeled.

In HMR, the laws of physics are the stable rules that make a complex physical reality possible. Living bodies are the vessels through which that intelligence experiences what it has made, and each human is one such instance.

Universal Intelligence

UI is the non-physical intelligence from which physical reality emerges, and the process by which it does so. It is what people have called God, Brahman, the Tao, or any higher power. HMR does not belong to any one religion. It offers a way of describing the same source that science can engage with.

In the Abrahamic traditions, God is complete and perfect from the start. In HMR, UI matures. It began without order and learned order.

From non-physical to physical, step by step

HMR's bridge to science goes thought by thought: if before the Big Bang there was a God and no space, what steps must happen, in order, to arrive at space?

  1. Me and not me. To focus at all, UI needs a first framework, and the first framework is identity. The smallest distinction needs two things: a point that is me, and a point imagined as not me.
  2. The first distance. Me and not me are apart, and that gap is the first distance anything ever had. In HMR, this unit corresponds to the Planck length.
  3. The boundary and pi. Testing the edge of me in every direction, with nothing to mark one direction as special, traces a circle in a plane and a sphere in space, and with it the number pi.
  4. Each new independent relation adds a dimension. A third thing off the line between the first two needs a new direction, and so on.
  5. The thought becomes physical. When the first complete model of itself closes, it ignites. In HMR, that is the Big Bang. Thinking in steps gives time, independent directions give space, and the stable rules of that exploration are the laws of physics.

What this does not do

It does not prove that UI exists, because no step is a measurement. It does not replace any established result of physics. It does not yet derive gravity.

Two models, side by side

What each includes, what each lacks, and what follows from each. These are HMR's views of both.

HMR's view

The materialist model

Includes matter and energy, the laws that describe them, and the scientific method.

Lacks, in HMR's view: an account of why anything exists, what consciousness is, and what a life is for. It also lacks a defined, fully healthy body to measure against, and a foundation for values.

Its technology and medicine are large, real, and established.

HMR's view

HMR

Includes a Universal Intelligence and our relationship to it, the body as a fascia-first tensegrity, and the physics linking the two. It also keeps science's method and its results.

Lacks, so far: finished mathematics that derives the physics we measure from its starting point, and completed tests. The first test, the simulation, is still being built.

If HMR is right, it gives a compass: a way to decide what to do moment to moment.
